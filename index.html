Combined Policy Imitation Learning on Specialized GA Bots

Train specialist GA experts, collect large-scale demonstrations, train expert networks, and combine them with a gating policy.

This project builds three genetic-algorithm specialists (Thruster, Shooter, Navigator), records millions of ticks of structured state-action data, trains per-expert neural imitation models, and produces a mixture-of-experts combined policy that blends specialist strengths.

Read overview Download report

Overview

Goal: produce an end-to-end imitation policy for X-Pilot that controls all four discrete actions (thrust, turnLeft, turnRight, shoot). Explore whether combining multiple specialized experts yields a stronger and more robust policy than single-expert imitation.

Benchmark

Genetic Algorithms trained and evaluated against a rule-based expert agent (Derin’s Expert).

Key outcome

The combined neural policy is smoother and outperforms the separate neural experts, while GA agents remain strong baselines.

Specialized GA Experts

Three specialized Genetic Algorithm agents were evolved to emphasize different behaviors while maintaining survival competency. A separate survivor GA was run first to seed robust survival behaviors into the specialist populations.

Survivor GA, purpose and impact

Before evolving specialists, a Survivor GA was trained to optimize longevity in the arena. The survivor produced reliable, high-survival chromosomes that were then injected into specialist GA populations as proven survivors. This seeding provided three benefits: faster convergence, fewer wall-crash degenerate runs, and a stable baseline so specialists could focus on their objective (thrusting, shooting, or navigation) without losing survival competence.

Data Collection

Large-scale per-tick logs were recorded while each GA expert competed against the rule-based agent. Logs contained raw variables and were normalized into 17 input features for training.

Rows collected

Shooter: 5.0M · Navigator: 5.5M · Thruster: 4.8M (≈15M total)

Recording protocol

Logs captured 21 raw variables per tick and were converted to 17 normalized inputs (range 0–1). Demonstrations span continuous competitive play for high-quality behavior coverage.

Inputs & outputs

Inputs: 17 structured features → Outputs: 4 discrete actions (thrust, turnLeft, turnRight, shoot).

Input features (17)

  • agent_tracking

Imitation Models

Per-expert neural networks were trained on each expert’s demonstrations. Architectures balance capacity and dataset size to capture behavior patterns.

Architectures

All expert networks use 4 hidden layers. The first hidden layer for Thruster and Shooter is 256 neurons, followed by 128 → 64 → 32. All layers are fully connected and output per-action probabilities.

Navigator had 5.5 million rows so Neural Network size had to be cut down to make it run. The first hidden layer for Navigator is 196 neurons, followed by 128 → 64 → 32. All layers are fully connected and output per-action probabilities.

Combined Policy (Mixture of Experts)

A gating network learns soft weights over the three experts. Each expert emits per-action probability vectors; the final action is the weighted sum of expert outputs. The result is a smooth, state-dependent blending of specialist behaviors.

Gating & blending

The gating network receives the same 17 inputs and outputs a softmax distribution [w₁,w₂,w₃]. Final action probabilities are a = w₁·a₁ + w₂·a₂ + w₃·a₃, then converted to discrete actions by argmax for deployment.

Behavioral effects

  • Smoother movement and decision patterns, less abrupt shooting than single-expert networks.
  • Stronger wall avoidance due to weighting of Navigator/Thruster when near obstacles.
  • Better overall action selection in mixed scenarios; combined policy outperforms each neural expert in most neural-vs-neural matches.

Combined policy demo

The combined policy inherits the navigation and wall-avoidance strengths of Navigator/Thruster and the precision of Shooter.

It behaves much smoother than individual neural experts (fewer abrupt shots, better obstacle avoidance).

Neural-vs-neural matches the combined model consistently outperforms the separate neural experts.

In direct GA-vs-GA comparisons, the original GAs still outperform the combined neural policy.

The combined policy can also occasionally over-emphasize Navigator in open spaces and adopt risky high-speed movement.

Summary

The combined neural policy produces smoother, more balanced behavior and consistently beats the separate neural experts. Compared to GA agents, the combined neural agent shows strong imitation performance but GAs still hold advantages againt Neural Networks.

The project also showed that it is quite possible to make very good Imitation bots if we train them on enough data.

Conclusions & Future Work

  1. Fine-tune the combined policy with reinforcement learning (RL) to close the gap to GA performance.
  2. Improve gating architectures (contextual gating, hierarchical gating) for sharper expert selection.
  3. Test transfer and robustness across maps, opponent types, and noisy inputs.
  4. Explore distillation of the mixture into a single compact network for lower-latency deployment.
